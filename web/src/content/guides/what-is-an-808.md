## The machine

Roland released the TR-808 Rhythm Composer in 1980. It made every sound with analogue circuits rather than recordings, so its drums sounded synthetic next to rival machines that played back samples of real drums. Production ended in 1983, after roughly 12,000 units. Cheap on the second-hand market, it was picked up by producers who loved exactly what reviewers had disliked: the deep, decaying kick, the snappy snare, the hand clap, the cowbell and the ticking hats.

Records such as Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" and Marvin Gaye's "Sexual Healing", both from 1982, made it famous. Electro, Miami bass, early hip hop and house all leaned on it.

## The bass

The 808's bass drum has a decay control. Turn it up and the kick becomes a long, falling sine-like tone with a definite pitch. Southern hip hop producers tuned it to play basslines, and trap made the tuned 808 the centre of the track: a short kick for the attack, then a long 808 note per bar or half-bar, often distorted so it is audible on small speakers, with glides between notes.

Today "808" in a sample pack almost always means this tuned bass sound, whether recorded from the machine, from a clone or synthesised from scratch.

## Using 808 samples

1. Pick an 808 with the character you want: clean and round, or distorted and gritty.
2. Find its root note and load it in a sampler, so the keyboard plays in tune.
3. Write the bassline in your key, leaving space for the kick.
4. Add glide between notes for movement, and saturation for presence on phones.
5. Check the low end in mono and on headphones.
