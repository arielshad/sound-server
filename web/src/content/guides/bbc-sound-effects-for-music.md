## What the archive is

The BBC Sound Effects archive puts tens of thousands of recordings from the BBC's own libraries online, including material from the Natural History Unit: animals, weather, transport, machines, crowds, interiors and decades of radio and television foley. Sound Server indexes about 33,000 of them, with WAV and MP3 downloads.

## What the RemArc licence allows

The recordings are offered under the BBC's RemArc licence, which permits use for **personal, educational and research purposes**. In practice that covers:

- practising sound design and production at home;
- school, college and university projects;
- research and archival study;
- personal videos and projects that don't earn money.

It does not cover selling or streaming music for royalties, monetised videos, client work, games or apps you sell, or advertising. Read the licence on the BBC's site for the exact wording; the archive also explains how to obtain a licence for commercial use.

## How Sound Server handles it

Every BBC result carries the verdict `no` ("Non-commercial only"), and the Commercial-safe switch hides them. The MCP server tells AI agents the same thing: BBC sounds may be suggested for personal or educational work, but never presented as cleared for a release.

## Alternatives for a release

Most kinds of sound in the BBC archive have openly licensed equivalents. Nature and city ambiences, rain, footsteps and machines are well covered by CC0 and CC BY recordings on Freesound and Wikimedia Commons, and by CC0 game audio packs.
