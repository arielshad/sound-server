## What the licence says

Sampling+ was designed for remix culture. Its deed summarises three permissions and limits:

1. **Sampling and transformation**: you may sample, mash up or otherwise creatively transform the work for any purpose, commercial included, **except advertising**.
2. **Sharing**: you may copy and distribute the whole, unchanged work, but **only non-commercially**.
3. **Attribution**: you must credit the original author.

The **NonCommercial Sampling+** variant adds a non-commercial condition to everything, so it rules out a commercial release.

## How it compares

| | CC BY | Sampling+ | NC Sampling+ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial track that transforms the sound | Yes | Yes | No |
| Use in an advert | Yes | No | No |
| Sell or share the raw sound commercially | Yes | No | No |
| Credit required | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Sound Server verdict | `yes-with-credit` | `restricted` | `no` |

## Why Sound Server says "restricted"

"Transform" is a judgement call, and a track can end up in an advert later, through a sync deal you didn't plan. That makes Sampling+ awkward for a release you want to license widely. Sound Server rates it `restricted`: usable, but read the conditions and decide whether they fit your plans. If they might not, pick a CC0 or CC BY sound instead; the Commercial-safe filter does this automatically.

## Where you'll meet it

Sampling+ was popular in the late 2000s with remix communities, especially ccMixter, and on Freesound, which offered it for uploads at the time. Older a cappellas and stems on ccMixter often carry it. The licence of an existing work doesn't change when a licence is retired, so these sounds remain under Sampling+.
