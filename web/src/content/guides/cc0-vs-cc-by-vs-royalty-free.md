## The short comparison

| | CC0 | CC BY | Royalty-free (site or store licence) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Use in a commercial track | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Credit required | No | Yes | Sometimes |
| Redistribute the raw sample | Yes | Yes, with credit | No |
| Who grants it | The creator, through Creative Commons | The creator, through Creative Commons | The site or store, under its own terms |
| Same terms everywhere | Yes | Yes | No: read each site's licence |
| Sound Server verdict | `yes` | `yes-with-credit` | `yes` or `yes-with-credit` |

## CC0: no strings

CC0 is a public-domain dedication. The creator waives copyright and related rights, so you can use, edit and release the sound without asking or crediting anyone. Kenney's game audio, many orchestral sample projects (such as VCSL) and a large share of Freesound are CC0. It is the simplest licence for a producer: there is nothing to track except, ideally, where the file came from.

## CC BY: credit, then do anything

CC BY (Attribution) allows any use, commercial included, as long as you credit the creator, name the work, link to the licence and indicate if you changed it. In music, "changed" is nearly always true, because you chop, pitch and process samples, so a credit that says "sampled" or "modified" is good practice. Version 4.0 lets you give credit "in any reasonable manner based on the medium", which for a song means the release notes, video description or credits page. See [how to credit CC BY samples](/guides/credit-cc-by-samples).

Watch for the add-ons. **BY-SA** (ShareAlike) would require your track to be released under the same licence, **BY-NC** forbids commercial use and **BY-ND** forbids changes. Those are not "CC BY" for release purposes.

## Royalty-free: use it, don't share it

Sample packs and sound effect sites usually write their own licences, and most follow the same pattern: you may use the sounds in your own productions, including commercial releases, without paying again; you may not resell, share or repackage the samples themselves, even for free. Some sites ask for credit (Zapsplat's free tier, for example), and many list uses they exclude, so the details differ from site to site.

Because each site's licence is different, Sound Server records the licence per source and shows its verdict on every result. Read the licence once for each site you rely on.

## Which to choose

For a release, any of the three works. CC0 is the least paperwork, CC BY needs a credit line kept with the project, and royalty-free sounds need nothing on the release but must never be shared raw. Avoid mixing in NC, SA or ND sounds unless the project will never be sold.
