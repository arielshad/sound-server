## Two sounds, one name

In drumming, a rimshot is a loud accent: the stick strikes the snare head and the metal rim at the same moment. A cross-stick, or side-stick, is the gentle click you hear in ballads and bossa nova. On drum machines, "rimshot" almost always means a short, tuned click, and that is what most producers search for.

This collection covers all three, so listen before you pick. Acoustic side-sticks bring a woody tone and some snare buzz. Machine rimshots are bright and very short, which makes them ideal for fast syncopated patterns.

## Using a rim in a groove

A rim works as a quiet backbeat in verses, with the full snare saved for the chorus. It also makes a good ghost-note layer between hats. Because rim samples are so short, small timing shifts matter: nudging them slightly late gives a laid-back feel, while early hits push the groove forward.
